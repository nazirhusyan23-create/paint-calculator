7 painting mistakes that ruin a finish

Most bad paint jobs aren't caused by bad paint — they're caused by one of these seven avoidable mistakes.

01

Wrong roller nap

A thick nap on a smooth wall leaves texture (orange peel); a thin nap on textured drywall misses the low spots. Match nap thickness to your wall's texture.

02

Painting in direct sunlight

Fast-drying paint in direct sun causes visible lap marks where wet paint meets a partially dried edge. Work in shade or on overcast conditions when possible.

03

Not "boxing" the paint

Mixing multiple cans together in one large bucket (boxing) evens out tiny batch-to-batch color variation. Skipping this is the most common cause of visible color banding on large walls.

04

Overloading the brush or roller

Too much paint causes drips and uneven buildup at edges. Load just enough that the tool doesn't drip when lifted, and unload excess on the tray's ridged section.

05

Recoating too soon

Applying a second coat before the first is properly dry drags the wet layer underneath, causing streaks and uneven sheen. Check the can's recoat time — don't estimate by touch alone.

06

Ignoring lighting when checking your work

Paint looks different under warm indoor bulbs than daylight. Check your finished wall under natural daylight before deciding it's finished — many missed spots only show up then.

07

Skipping the "cutting in" order

Painting edges (cutting in) after rolling the main wall area creates a visible texture difference where the two meet. Cut in edges first, then roll the wall while the cut-in paint is still wet, to blend the two seamlessly.

Common questions

FAQ

What's the single most common DIY paint mistake? +

Skipping wall prep (cleaning, patching, sanding) is the single biggest cause of poor results — even expensive paint won't hide dust, grease, or uneven patches. See our wall prep checklist for the full process.

How do I fix visible lap marks after the fact? +

Once fully cured, a full additional coat applied evenly (working quickly, out of direct sun, with a consistent wet edge) will usually cover lap marks from the coat underneath.
