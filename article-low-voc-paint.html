Low-VOC and zero-VOC paint: is it worth it?

The label sounds like marketing, but the difference is real and measurable — here's when it actually matters for your project.

What VOCs actually are

VOCs (volatile organic compounds) are chemicals that evaporate from paint as it dries, causing the "new paint smell." Some VOCs can cause headaches, eye irritation, or respiratory discomfort in sensitive individuals, particularly in poorly ventilated spaces during and shortly after painting.

Standard latex paint typically contains 50-150 grams of VOCs per liter. "Low-VOC" paint is generally under 50 g/L, and "zero-VOC" is under 5 g/L (though the base paint may still rise slightly once colorant is added).

Why it costs more

Reformulating paint to reduce VOCs while keeping durability, coverage, and washability comparable requires different binders and solvents, which typically adds 10-20% to the price per gallon.

Worth the extra cost when...

When low-VOC actually matters

Nurseries and children's bedrooms: Lower odor and lower chemical exposure during the first weeks after painting, when off-gassing is highest.

Poorly ventilated rooms: Basements, small bathrooms, or rooms with no openable windows benefit most, since fumes can't disperse quickly.

Anyone with respiratory sensitivity: Asthma or chemical sensitivity make low-VOC worth the premium regardless of room.

Less critical for: Garages, sheds, or exterior work with good ventilation, where the cost premium buys less practical benefit.

Common questions

FAQ

Does low-VOC paint cover as well as regular paint? +

Most modern low-VOC paints from major brands perform comparably in coverage and durability — the gap that existed a decade ago has mostly closed. Check reviews for the specific product line you're considering.

Does the tint/colorant added at the store affect VOC level? +

Yes — colorant can add a small amount of VOCs even to a zero-VOC base, especially with darker or more saturated colors. If VOC level is a strict requirement, ask your paint store for their colorant's VOC contribution.
