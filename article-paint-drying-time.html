How long does paint take to dry and cure?

"Dry" and "cured" are not the same thing — mixing them up is the most common reason freshly painted walls get dented or marked.

Dry time

This is how long it takes for the paint's surface to become touch-dry and ready for a second coat. For most latex/acrylic wall paint, that's roughly 1-4 hours, though thicker or darker colors can take longer. This is the number printed on most paint cans as "recoat time."

Cure time

Curing is the slower process of the paint film fully hardening all the way through, not just on the surface. This is when the paint reaches its full washability and scratch resistance. Latex paint typically cures in 2-4 weeks, even though it feels completely dry within a day.

This is why painters recommend waiting before scrubbing walls, hanging heavy items, or pushing furniture directly against a freshly painted surface — the paint looks finished long before it actually is.

Typical timeframes

Dry and cure time by paint type

Latex/acrylic wall paint: Dry to touch in 1 hour, recoat in 4 hours, fully cured in 2-4 weeks.

Oil-based paint: Dry to touch in 6-8 hours, recoat in 24 hours, fully cured in 7-30 days.

High-humidity conditions: Add 25-50% to all of the above — moisture in the air slows evaporation significantly.

Cold rooms (below 50°F / 10°C): Both dry and cure time extend substantially, and some paints won't cure properly at all below their minimum temperature.

Common questions

FAQ

What is the difference between dry time and cure time? +

Dry time is how long until paint is touch-dry and safe for a second coat, usually 1-4 hours for latex paint. Cure time is how long the paint takes to fully harden and reach maximum durability, which can take 2-4 weeks even though the surface looks and feels dry much sooner.

How long before I can put furniture back against a painted wall? +

Wait at least 24-48 hours before placing furniture against a freshly painted wall, and up to a full week for anything that presses firmly against the surface, since the paint is still curing and can mark easily.
