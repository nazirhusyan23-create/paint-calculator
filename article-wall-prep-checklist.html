How to prep a wall before painting

Most paint jobs fail on prep, not paint. Twenty minutes of prep per wall prevents peeling, uneven color, and visible patches later.

Six steps, in order

The full prep checklist

01

Clear and cover

Move furniture away from walls, remove wall hangings, and lay drop cloths over floors and remaining furniture.

02

Clean the walls

Wipe down with a damp sponge and mild detergent. Grease and dust both prevent paint from bonding properly — kitchens and bathrooms need this step most.

03

Patch holes and cracks

Fill nail holes, dents, and cracks with spackle or joint compound. Let it dry fully (usually 1-2 hours) before sanding.

04

Sand smooth

Lightly sand patched spots and any glossy areas with fine-grit sandpaper (150-220 grit) so new paint has something to grip.

05

Tape the edges

Apply painter's tape along trim, ceiling lines, switch plates, and outlets. Press it down firmly to stop paint from bleeding underneath.

06

Prime where needed

Prime over patched spots, stains, new drywall, or when making a major color change (especially dark to light). Skip it for simple same-color refreshes.

Avoid these

Common prep mistakes

Painting over wet patches. Spackle needs to fully dry (check the product label) or it will crack and telegraph through the new paint.

Skipping the sponge-down. Dust from sanding alone is often enough to cause a patchy, uneven finish if walls aren't wiped after.

Removing tape too late. Pull painter's tape while the paint is still slightly tacky (not fully dry) for the cleanest line — fully cured paint can peel off with the tape.

Common questions

FAQ

Do I really need to prime every wall? +

No — priming is mainly needed for new drywall, stained or repaired areas, or a significant color change. A same-color refresh over a clean, sound wall usually doesn't need it.

How long should I wait after patching before painting? +

Most spackle and joint compound products need 1-2 hours to dry for small patches, or up to 24 hours for deeper repairs. Check your specific product's label — painting over wet compound is the most common cause of visible patch marks.
