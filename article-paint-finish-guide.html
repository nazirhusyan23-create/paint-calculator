Paint finish guide: which sheen goes where

Sheen affects durability, cleanability, and how forgiving a wall is of imperfections — not just how shiny it looks. Here's how to pick correctly the first time.

Matte / Flat

The least reflective finish, which means it hides wall imperfections (dents, patches, uneven drywall) better than any other sheen. The tradeoff is durability — matte paint scuffs and marks more easily, and it's harder to wipe clean without burnishing the surface. Best for adult bedrooms, formal living rooms, and ceilings.

Eggshell

A slight sheen — named for its resemblance to an actual eggshell's texture. It hides imperfections almost as well as matte while being noticeably easier to wipe down. This is the most common "all-purpose" interior wall finish for hallways, dining rooms, and living areas.

Satin

A soft, pearl-like sheen that's genuinely washable — you can scrub satin-finish walls without damaging the paint. It shows more wall imperfections than eggshell, so it's worth a skim coat on older or patched walls first. Common in kitchens, bathrooms, kids' rooms, and hallways with heavy traffic.

Semi-Gloss

Noticeably shiny and highly durable, semi-gloss resists moisture and stands up to frequent scrubbing. It highlights every wall flaw, so it's rarely used on large wall surfaces — instead it's the standard choice for trim, doors, cabinets, and bathroom walls.

Gloss / High-Gloss

The shiniest, most durable, and most moisture-resistant finish, but also the least forgiving — it shows brush strokes and surface imperfections clearly. Typically reserved for front doors, furniture, and accent trim rather than full walls.

Quick reference

Recommended sheen by room

Bedrooms & formal living rooms: Matte or eggshell — low traffic, imperfection-hiding matters more than washability.

Kitchens & bathrooms: Satin or semi-gloss — moisture and grease resistance, easy to wipe down.

Hallways & kids' rooms: Satin — heavy traffic and frequent marks need a washable surface.

Trim, doors & cabinets: Semi-gloss or gloss — durability and a crisp, defined look against wall color.

Ceilings: Flat/matte, almost always — reduces glare and hides seams.

Common questions

FAQ

Can I mix sheens in the same room? +

Yes — it's standard practice. Walls typically get eggshell or satin, while trim, doors, and cabinets get semi-gloss for contrast and durability.

Does sheen affect how much paint I need? +

Coverage rates are similar across sheens (usually 350–400 sq ft/gallon), though gloss finishes sometimes need an extra coat for even sheen consistency. Use the paint calculator with your specific can's listed coverage for the most accurate number.
