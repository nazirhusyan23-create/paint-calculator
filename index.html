Before you buy

Coverage tips that actually save money

Textured or unprimed walls drink more paint. Drop your coverage input to 250–300 sq ft/gallon on stucco, popcorn ceilings, or bare drywall.

Dark-to-light color changes usually need 3 coats, not 2 — adjust the "coats" field accordingly before you buy.

Buy the next full gallon up, never round down. Matching a paint batch mid-project rarely comes out identical.

Ceilings are a separate calculation. Run this tool again using ceiling length × width as "length" and "width," with height and door/window fields set to 0.

Common questions

FAQ

How many square feet does one gallon of paint cover? +

Most standard latex or acrylic wall paints cover 350–400 square feet per gallon on a single coat over primed drywall. Textured, porous, or unprimed surfaces reduce coverage to roughly 250–300 square feet per gallon — check your specific can's label, since it varies by brand and finish.

Do I need two coats of paint? +

Two coats are recommended for most color changes, new drywall, or when covering a darker existing color. A single coat is usually enough only when repainting the same or a very similar color over a previously painted, well-sealed wall.

How much should I subtract for doors and windows? +

A standard interior door opening removes about 21 square feet from your wall area, and a standard window removes about 15 square feet. This calculator subtracts those automatically based on how many you enter.

Should I round up or down when buying paint? +

Always round up to the nearest full gallon or quart. Running short mid-job risks a visible color mismatch between paint batches, while leftover paint is useful for future touch-ups.
